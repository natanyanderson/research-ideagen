# Related Work

## 2. Related Work

**Scientific Idea Generation.** Recent work has demonstrated that LLMs can produce research ideas competitive with human experts on novelty metrics (Si et al., 2024), though systematic evaluation remains challenging. Wang et al. (2023) propose SciMON, which generates novel research directions by identifying gaps between topic embeddings and a literature corpus—an approach our work builds on. A key limitation of both systems is their reliance on a single novelty signal: semantic distance from existing work. Our dual novelty scoring addresses this directly by separately assessing whether an idea’s method and its application domain are both novel, avoiding false positives from domain-transfer ideas that appear novel by similarity alone.

**Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Science.** Our pipeline follows the RAG paradigm established for scientific question answering (Skarlinski et al., 2023), adapting it for the ideation task rather than QA. Rather than retrieving evidence to answer a question, we retrieve literature context to ground and constrain generation. Wadden et al. (2022) demonstrate that domain-specific embeddings substantially improve retrieval quality for scientific text; our use of `text-embedding-3-small` reflects a pragmatic tradeoff between retrieval quality and deployment cost for a prototype system.

**Novelty Assessment.** Prior frameworks for measuring research novelty have emphasized its multi-dimensional nature—arguing that novelty along methodological, conceptual, and empirical axes must be assessed independently (Verhoeven et al., 2016). Novielli et al. (2020) show that automated systems can classify contribution types in research papers with reasonable accuracy. Our work operationalizes a similar insight: similarity-based and application-based novelty are distinct dimensions that can diverge, and conflating them leads to systematic misclassification of domain-transfer ideas. To our knowledge, no prior automated ideation system explicitly decouples these two signals.
