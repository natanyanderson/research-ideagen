# Related Work Papers

6 papers for the related work section.

## 1. Training AI Co-Scientists Using Rubric Rewards
- **Relevance**: Closest to our work. Uses rubric-based RL to train LMs to generate research plans.
- **Difference**: They focus on planning, we focus on ideation and evaluation. They don't address novelty scoring.
- **Similarity**: 0.371

## 2. Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas? (Si et al., 2024)
- **Relevance**: Directly studies LLM research ideation quality
- **Difference**: They do blind review comparison, we focus on automated evaluation
- **Key finding**: LLM ideas rated as more novel but less feasible than human ideas

## 3. The AI Scientist (Lu et al., 2024)
- **Relevance**: End-to-end automated research system
- **Difference**: Much broader scope (generates full papers). We focus specifically on the ideation and evaluation components.

## 4. SciMON: Scientific Inspiration Machines Optimized for Novelty
- **Relevance**: Automated scientific idea generation using literature
- **Difference**: They optimize for novelty using retrieval. We add the dual scoring to better define what counts as novel.

## 5. Creativity Support Tools: A Review
- **Relevance**: Background on how tools affect human creativity
- **Key point**: Tools can both enhance and constrain creative processes — relevant to our anchoring hypothesis

## 6. Anchoring and Adjustment in Judgment (Tversky & Kahneman)
- **Relevance**: Foundational work on anchoring effects
- **Key point**: Initial information systematically biases subsequent judgments — the theoretical basis for our experiment
