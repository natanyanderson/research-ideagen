# Abstract

Large language models show promise as tools for research ideation, but evaluating the novelty of generated ideas remains an open problem. Existing approaches rely on similarity-based methods that conflate two distinct forms of novelty: methodological novelty and application novelty. We present a research ideation pipeline that decouples these signals through a dual novelty scoring approach, combining corpus similarity with an LLM-based judgment of application novelty. In a controlled user study (N=20), we find that AI-assisted ideation significantly increases application novelty (Mann-Whitney U = 22, p = 0.018, r = 0.51) but not methodological novelty (p = 0.21)—suggesting the pipeline broadens the domain space researchers explore rather than altering their methodological reasoning. This dissociation, only detectable through our dual scoring framework, constitutes the paper’s central empirical contribution. Supporting pipeline evaluations across 50 generated ideas show that 18% would have been incorrectly rejected by similarity-based filtering alone, and 32% scored higher on application novelty than on similarity. We further find that AI-assisted ideation reduces variance in novelty scores, functioning as a floor-raiser rather than a ceiling-setter for idea quality. We describe the pipeline architecture, present qualitative case studies, and discuss anchoring effects observed in the AI-assisted group as a target for future investigation.
