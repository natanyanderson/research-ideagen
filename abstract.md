# Abstract

Large language models show promise as tools for research ideation, but evaluating the novelty of generated ideas remains an open problem. Existing approaches rely on similarity-based methods that measure distance between generated ideas and an embedding of the scientific corpus. This approach conflates two distinct forms of novelty: methodological novelty and application novelty. We present a research ideation pipeline that decouples these signals through a dual novelty scoring approach, combining corpus similarity with an LLM-based judgment of application novelty. Across 50 generated ideas spanning 10 research topics, 18% would have been incorrectly rejected by similarity-based filtering alone, and 32% scored higher on application novelty than on similarity. We describe the pipeline architecture, present qualitative case studies including a domain-transfer example in agricultural reinforcement learning, and outline a planned user study (N=20) investigating whether AI-assisted ideation mitigates anchoring effects in human researchers.
